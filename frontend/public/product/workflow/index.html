From site observation to closeout

Start with the evidence available on site. Keep later decisions with the change so the project and commercial teams can see what has been agreed, paid and resolved.

See a worked example

Five handoffs, one record

Use the project agreement for authority and notice. Add each record when the responsible team has the facts.

Capture the condition

Save what the site user observed, where and when it happened, who recorded it and the evidence available.

Record direction and notice

Identify who can instruct work under the agreement. Record the issued instruction, notice dispatch and receipt as separate facts.

Track scope, work and price

Record agreement on scope, price and time alongside permission to proceed and the work the crew performed.

Certify and reconcile

Record signed certificates, retention and receipt allocations. Keep unpaid or disputed amounts visible.

Close the case

Confirm the work and financial resolution. Keep earlier records available and reopen the affected balance if new evidence changes it.

When the drawing and the site disagree

In the worked example, a beam blocks the proposed duct route. The site team opens a record with a location and a photo, then links the instruction and the accepted quotation when they arrive.

When a price is still open

Keep the issued direction and the affected scope in the case. When the quote is agreed, link its signed version and the scope it covers. If one line remains disputed, keep that line visible while the rest of the case moves forward.

When the work is finished

Compare the signed certificate with the measured work. Read the retention and the allocated receipts separately, then check what still needs to be resolved before closeout.

What to bring to the first review

Pick one live change. Bring the records you already have and identify who can supply the rest.

  • The location and an observation of what the crew found
  • Any photo, drawing, field note or written instruction already on hand
  • The project agreement or the person who can confirm authority and notice rules
  • The signed quotation, certificate or receipt if the change has reached that stage
